# Usage Story: Understanding Conflicting Sightings

During the fictional Cedar Watch exercise, Observation Team Cedar reports six cargo trucks associated with the fictional Red Falcon force at Cedar Junction at 08:20. An imagery review describes six trucks at 08:42. In the conflicting-count scenario, Observation Team Cedar reports six cargo trucks at East Depot at 09:20, while the Imagery Review Desk reports four at 09:45.

In Commander mode, the application opens with a plain-language situation overview: the East Depot reports describe the same episode but give different counts at different observation times. A grouped range-and-dot plot displays each source-reported count without adding them together or claiming a verified inventory. The user selects “Compare reports” to see the source names, reported quantities, observation times, receipt times, and comparison notes.

The user selects “Inspect evidence.” The inspector distinguishes what a source reported from what is known: the actor attribution is suspected, the reports were made 25 minutes apart, and the available evidence does not establish why the counts differ. The report count is not a count of distinct vehicles.

An analyst opens the same scenario in Analyst mode and examines the chronological report list, observation-to-receipt timeline, and fictional-grid plot. The evidence inspector adds source confidence, validation status, source independence, translation status, missingness, and the processing trail. A separate scenario demonstrates a delayed personnel report at North Ridge; another shows a translated, ambiguous count range near Pine Crossing with human verification incomplete.

Both users can explain what the reports say, where they differ, and what remains unknown. The application supports interpretation of synthetic evidence; it does not verify reports, estimate total inventory, prioritize targets, or recommend operational action.
